AI co-doctor.

A clinical colleague for places where doctors, tests and specialists are scarce. It follows the illness over days, plans diagnosis, treatment and referral with what is actually available, and helps clinicians sharpen their own skills.

View systems Study with us
The aim

A companion that, used by district clinicians and community health workers with only local resources, shortens the time to correct treatment, cuts missed serious illness and needless referral, and raises the clinician’s own skill. Routine cases should work offline.

How we measure it

Time to correct treatment, missed serious illness, needless referrals and tests, the clinician’s unaided accuracy over time, follow-up outcome, and cost per patient.

Where we work

Emergency medicine benchmarks, paediatric cancer in Vietnam, and district-level care in low-resource health systems.

What we have built

A diagnostic panel, and an evaluation you can trust.

Diagnostic panel

Reasons like a team of physicians

Our own panel of simulated physicians orders tests and reasons step by step, with accuracy comparable to published results on emergency-medicine cases.

Faithful evaluation

Measuring what the evidence supports

We found and fixed a flaw in how diagnostic benchmarks simulate the patient, so a score reflects the evidence that actually exists.

Safety layer

Knows when to hand off

Calibrated error signals and an independent second reader flag the cases a clinician should see first.

Next target

Earlier recognition of childhood cancer in Vietnam.

Late diagnosis is the problem. The question for clinicians: at a child’s first visit, would the system have raised cancer, asked for the right next test, and referred, and how much earlier than actually happened?

First result

On 20 unseen published cases, the adapted system raised all 11 cancers by their diagnosis visit, and flagged 1 of 6 benign look-alikes.

Plan
  • A silent, retrospective study on de-identified records, with no patient contact.
  • Success criteria agreed with oncologists and fixed before results are seen.
  • Setting cards for commune, district, provincial and national hospitals, so advice matches the tests each can do.
  • Built with hospital partners and ethics approval.
The design

Seven properties of a co-doctor.

The diagnostic panel is the reasoning core of a larger system.

Follows the illness

Treat, wait, re-test, revise, across days rather than one visit.

Perceives

Listens in local languages; reads rapid-test strips, rashes and printouts from a phone.

Plans the pathway

Diagnosis, treatment, referral and follow-up as one decision, against local tests, drugs and transport.

Teaches

Asks before it answers, and succeeds when the clinician needs it less.

Learns locally

Follow-up outcomes update district disease rates and the system’s own calibration.

Knows when to hand off

Calibrated, cites evidence, refers early, with an audit trail.

Works where the patient is

Offline for routine cases, cloud for hard ones, a voice interface for community health workers.
