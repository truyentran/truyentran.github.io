AI Future

Civilisational AI and the science of intelligence.

What determines whether a population of increasingly intelligent agents becomes a productive, adaptive and resilient civilisation, or an unstable and highly concentrated one? We are building a science of civilisational intelligence: how humans, AI systems and institutions collectively learn, coordinate, and adapt.

Study with us View systems
Why it matters

As AI becomes more capable and autonomous, the unit of intelligence that matters may be the network of interacting minds, tools, institutions and environments, not the individual model. Single-model benchmarks do not say how such systems perform, conflict, or stay accountable.

Working hypothesis

Collective intelligence is not set by individual intelligence alone. It emerges from capability, diversity, communication, memory, institutions, incentives, resources and scale acting together.

Programme

CIVITAS-AI is a three-year research programme planned for 2027–2029 at the Applied Artificial Intelligence Institute, Deakin University.

CIVITAS-AI

Three scientific aims and one laboratory.

The emphasis is on falsifiable, transferable, explanatory principles, not another generic multi-agent simulator.

Aim 1

Laws of collective intelligence

When do collectives scale, saturate or fail as population, capability and organisation grow? Theories are fitted on some agent populations and tested on withheld ones, against matched-resource baselines.

Aim 2

Dynamics of artificial civilisations

Causal mechanisms of institution formation, cooperation and cheating, concentration of power and knowledge, collapse and recovery in societies of autonomous agents.

Aim 3

Human–AI institutions

How to bring AI into group decisions while measuring human influence, accountability and epistemic independence, not just decision accuracy.

Enabler

CIVITAS Lab

A reproducible laboratory for theory tests, controlled interventions and independent replication, reusing existing platforms where they fit.

First experiments
  • Collective scaling: predict the effect of population, capability and communication under matched budgets. This comes first.
  • Diversity: when do heterogeneous weaker agents beat homogeneous stronger ones?
  • Institutional emergence: when do locally evolved rules persist and outperform fixed ones?
  • Failure and recovery: susceptibility to cheating, collusion and misinformation.
  • Human agency: decision quality and actual human influence, measured separately.
How we hold ourselves to account
  • Baselines matched on compute, model calls, tools and information.
  • Causal interventions, not correlations.
  • Predictions written down before withheld tests are run.
  • Findings repeated across model families, including non-LLM agents.
  • Protocols and resources released so others can replicate.
Science of intelligence

Foundations that run alongside.

Physics of intelligence

First-principles accounts of intelligence as a physical, non-equilibrium process involving energy, memory, prediction, control, abstraction and structure transfer.

Low-energy and operator-first AI

Alternatives to scale-heavy AI, judged by adaptation cost as well as capability.

Embodiment

Embodied multisensory intelligence and world-coupled cognition.
